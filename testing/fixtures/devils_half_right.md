# Devil's Half Right

[CUE: straight into the lens]

The Devil got it half right. What do I mean? Simply that the devil quoted the Bible to Jesus — Word for word — And he got it exactly half right.

Matthew chapter four. The devil takes Jesus to the pinnacle of the temple and says, "If You are the Son of God, throw Yourself down. For it is written: 'He will command His angels concerning You, and they will lift You up in their hands, so that You will not strike Your foot against a stone.'"

That's Psalm ninety-one. He quoted it correctly. Every word.

Here's the half he left on the table. Read a few verses further, and God says of the one who trusts him, "When he calls out to Me, I will answer him; I will be with him in trouble. I will deliver him and honor him." The old Greek translation says, "I will rescue him and glorify him."

[CUE: slower]

See what the devil did? He offered glory now, for one spectacular jump. The psalm he was quoting promised glory from God, in God's time, to the one who trusts him. I haven't found a commentator who points that out. But it's right there in the psalm.

And watch how Jesus answers. Not with an argument. With the rest of the Bible: "It is also written: 'Do not put the Lord your God to the test.'"

It is also written. A verse pulled loose from its psalm can be aimed at anything. Jesus answers Scripture with Scripture.

The devil isn't the only one who has quoted the Bible and missed the point. Jesus told men who knew their Scriptures thoroughly, "You pore over the Scriptures because you presume that by them you possess eternal life. These are the very words that testify about Me, yet you refuse to come to Me to have life."

It wasn't that they didn't know the text. It was the glory they were after while they read it.

[CUE: pause, then to the lens]

The devil can quote the verse. He just won't finish the psalm. Knowing the verse was never the test. Doing the Father's will with it is.
